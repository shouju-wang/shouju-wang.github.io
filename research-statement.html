Research statement

Designing AI that respects the context around it.

Shouju Wang · University of Hawai‘i at Mānoa

Overview

My research centers on building trustworthy and privacy-preserving AI systems, with a focus on large language model (LLM) agents that operate in socially complex, real-world environments. I am particularly interested in understanding and enforcing contextual integrity: the idea that information flows should respect the norms of the contexts in which they occur.

Research themes

LLM privacy & contextual integrity

As LLM-powered agents become embedded in everyday workflows—scheduling meetings, drafting emails, and managing personal data—they inevitably encounter sensitive information. My work investigates how these agents can be designed and evaluated to respect privacy norms grounded in contextual-integrity theory.

I have developed benchmarks and mitigation strategies for realistic privacy evaluation of LLM agents, resulting in work published at EMNLP 2025 Findings (Privacy in Action). My ongoing project, MPCI-Bench, extends this inquiry to multimodal settings through pairwise contextual-integrity evaluation for LLM and VLM agents.

Agent alignment & safety

Beyond privacy, I am broadly interested in aligning autonomous agents: ensuring that their behavior reflects human values and intentions across diverse interaction contexts. This includes studying how agents handle conflicting instructions, navigate ambiguous social norms, and balance helpfulness with safety constraints.

I believe that grounding alignment in well-established social theories such as contextual integrity offers a principled way to move beyond one-size-fits-all safety rules.

Fair graph learning

My earlier work addressed fairness in graph-based machine learning, specifically anomaly detection. I developed DECAF-GAD, a causal-inference framework that mitigates bias in graph-neural-network anomaly detectors while preserving detection accuracy. This work was presented as an oral paper at ECAI 2025, and it reflects my broader commitment to building AI systems that are both effective and equitable.

Looking ahead

I aim to develop formal frameworks and practical tools that help AI agents reason about and respect information norms in complex, multimodal settings.

How can autonomous agents perceive, reason about, and act on multimodal information while preserving the social norms that make information use appropriate?

I envision AI agents that understand not only what information they can access, but also whether and how it is appropriate to use it. Reaching that goal requires advances in norm-aware reasoning, privacy-preserving architectures, and rigorous evaluation methods.
